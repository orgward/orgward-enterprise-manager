# OrgWard: intended complete product

OrgWard is a governed enterprise design and operating system, not just an
organisation chart, chatbot, workflow catalogue or software-generation demo.
Its purpose is to keep what an enterprise intends, how it is organised, what
it actually does and the evidence of its outcomes connected as it changes.

## What a customer should be able to do

1. **Describe or import the business.** Start with conversation for a new
   enterprise, or discover and migrate an existing one. Capture strategy,
   customers/offerings/economics, capabilities/processes, people/agents, authority,
   resources, information/systems, governance/risks/controls, measures and lifecycle.
   Include informal, exceptional, manual and physical work, not only logged IT work.
2. **Explore and redesign it coherently.** Use linked graph, list, form and matrix
   views across sixteen perspectives and increasing levels of detail. Edit from
   any authorized entry point, branch, compare, review, merge and publish versions.
   See dependencies, conflicts, constraints and affected operating work before
   committing a change. Every view refers to the same canonical identities.
3. **Operate real work.** Turn processes/cases into durable human, agent,
   deterministic-software and external-system work. Assign instructions, tools,
   authority, budgets and resources; receive typed outputs and measured outcomes.
   Pause, intervene, approve, revise and recover without losing history or
   bypassing checkpoints. Unsupported automation stays visible.
4. **Create and evolve supporting software.** Refine business design into systems,
   requirements, architecture and executable SDLC plans. Agents can make real
   repository changes, build/test, evaluate against intent, produce artifacts,
   request independent approval, deploy to authorized targets and recover.
   Design/evidence changes invalidate stale context and approvals. Observed
   software outcomes feed proposed business corrections, not automatic truth.
5. **Govern and continuously check the enterprise.** Sentinel ingests sources,
   proposes claims, compiles accepted knowledge and detects contradictions/drift.
   Warden enforces current policy at actions; Arbiter handles accountable decisions;
   Steward owns data meaning/quality/lineage; Ledger records verifiable evidence;
   Overseer supervises bounded agent work. These share identity and execution
   infrastructure instead of becoming disconnected mini-products.
6. **Adopt and own it as an enterprise.** Managed isolated SaaS or customer-owned
   installation, enterprise identity, scoped support, residency, quotas, backups,
   tested restore, upgrades, coexistence migration, cutover, export and exit.
   Existing ERP/CRM/specialist systems may remain authoritative integrations.
   Extensible typed industry concepts do not bypass the shared constraints.

## The outcome, and its honest boundary

A customer should be able to represent its entire reviewed business scope,
identify what is runnable/tested/successful, operate supported work and change
any part while preserving links, constraints and causal history across the whole
enterprise. Missing knowledge, unsupported actions and stale evidence have an
owner and next action; they must not disappear behind a green dashboard.

This does not promise native replacement of every enterprise application,
automatic lawful authority, autonomous physical activity without a qualified
performer/adapter, or knowledge of undiscovered future business behavior.
“100 percent” always names a reviewed scope, denominator and evidence date.
The plan is fulfilled only by working UI/API/backend, real operating journeys,
independent review and release evidence—not by document counts or model choice.

## A concrete customer journey

A repair business imports its existing catalogue and describes exceptions in
chat. It sees customers, offerings, staff, tools, suppliers, processes and controls
as linked views. Its owner changes who may approve a repair: OrgWard previews
affected work, forms, policies and software, then publishes a reviewed version.
Workers use real inspection forms; a bounded agent drafts a quotation; a human
approves the protected step. Where the customer portal must change, SDLC derives
requirements/evals from the design, builds and tests the change, obtains approval
and deploys it. Sentinel detects any implementation drift. Results and failures
return as evidence and proposed improvements. Migration/restore/export retain
the business identities, history and outstanding obligations throughout.
